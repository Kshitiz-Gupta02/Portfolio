Bain Case Competition Network · 2023

BrAINWARS — FluorCo Growth Strategy

Why a 50-year-old acid business had stopped growing, and how to fix it.

Problem

FluorCo made hydrofluoric acid for 50 years. The company overall was growing 40% a year, but the HF business had gone completely flat.

Approach

We split the business into two issue trees — market and internal — and ran the actual numbers. FluorCo was paying $38,900 per case for raw material shipped from Vietnam and Canada, while competitors sourced cheaper fluorspar from South Africa for $25,000–30,000. Plant utilization was also swinging between 37% and 64%, with 60% of sales stuck in South India.

Outcome

We proposed new suppliers, expansion into new regions, and using queuing math to push utilization to 90%. Cleared Round 1 and reached the Semi-Finals — top 20 teams nationally.

Financial Accounting · Course Project

Godrej Consumer Products — Financial Analysis

Eleven years of statements, pulled apart to find what was really driving the returns.

Problem

A single year's balance sheet doesn't tell you whether a company's returns are real or just lucky. I wanted to know what was actually behind Godrej Consumer's ~19–24% Return on Equity.

Approach

I ran horizontal, vertical, and ratio analysis on GCPL's standalone statements from FY2012 to FY2022, then broke ROE down using DuPont's formula — margin, asset turnover, and leverage — to see which one was actually doing the work.

Outcome

Almost none of it was debt — GCPL has been nearly debt-free since 2014. I caught a one-off ₹282 crore tax credit that had inflated 2019's numbers, flagged margin pressure from rising input costs in FY21–22, and built a 2-year forecast projecting ROE settling around 18.5–19.5%.

Operations Research · Course Project

Ambulance Routing via Graph Theory

Shaving real minutes off ambulance response times using shortest-path math.

Problem

In emergencies, minutes matter — but ambulance routing usually comes down to driver instinct, not math.

Approach

I modelled the road network as a weighted directed graph — junctions and hospitals as nodes, travel time as edge weight — then ran Dijkstra's algorithm to find the fastest route there and back, accounting for one-way streets. For simultaneous calls, I added bipartite matching so total delay across all trips stayed as low as possible.

Outcome

On a sample network, the optimized routes cut a 20-minute round trip to 18 — a 10% saving that compounds fast when lives are on the line. I also set up 8 KPIs so a dispatch team could keep tracking performance afterward.

Growth Central VC · 23 months

KPI Systems — Three Iterations

Rebuilding how a 200-person company measured performance, until it actually worked.

Problem

The company measured people by counting finished tasks. That rewarded busywork over results, and it was quietly causing friction between teammates.

Approach

V1 established baseline metrics like satisfaction and time-to-hire. V2 added productivity ratios, which backfired — people argued over candidate quality before even interviewing, since sourcing effort wasn't counted. V3 fixed it with one formula (Total Score = 5A + 2B − 6C + 10D − 4E) that rewarded job posts and hires, penalized unselected candidates, and capped shortlists.

Outcome

Rolled out across 5 teams. Productivity rose 60%, absenteeism dropped 40%, and retention climbed from 10% to 40%.

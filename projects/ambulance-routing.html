Course project: DOM208 Operations Research, Shiv Nadar University (2022) -10% (simulated sample)

Ambulance Routing Network via Graph Theory

Applied Dijkstra shortest-path algorithms and bipartite matching to a sample road network with a single hospital base to minimize emergency transit times.

Executive Summary

The Problem
Traditional dispatch relied on driver intuition and habit rather than mathematical network optimization, losing critical minutes.
My Role
Formulated a sample road network with a single hospital base as a directed graph, applied Dijkstra shortest-path algorithm, and evaluated dispatch routing.
Core Tools
Graph Theory, Dijkstra Algorithm, Bipartite Matching
Primary Outcome
Cut sample round-trip response time from 20 minutes to 18 minutes (-10% in simulated sample) for one sample case (patient P2) in the sample road network.

1. Graph Formulation & Directional Weighting

The sample road network was formulated as a directed graph G = (V, E) with a single hospital as the base, where intersections represented vertices and road segments represented directed edges with travel time weights. Separate onward and return journeys were calculated to account for one-way street constraints for one patient and one round trip at a time.

Deep Dive: Mathematical Formulation & Project Scope (Click to expand)

Course project for DOM208 Operations Research at Shiv Nadar University (November 2022). The paper's scope is a single hospital as the base and one patient and one round trip at a time in a sample road network.

Dijkstra's algorithm executes with O((V + E) log V) time complexity using a min-priority queue to identify the minimum-weight directed path. In a separate section of the paper, bipartite matching is used for the assignment logic when multiple patients and ambulances are considered.

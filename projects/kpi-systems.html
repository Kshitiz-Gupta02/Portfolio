Operations & Product Growth Central VC 23-Month Redesign

KPI Systems — Three Iterations

Noticing that performance was measured purely by task count, and rebuilding the model three times until it actually balanced sourcing effort, hiring quality, and team morale.

RoleSelf-initiated, later approved company-wide
Scope1 company-wide model + 3 HR-specific iterations

Context

When I started as a Core Team Member, performance was tracked by counting what tasks a person did and how many times — a proxy that said nothing about the effort behind the work or the impact it created. I began remodelling performance management as a side project to fix that.

My Role

I designed and built every version of the model myself. I pitched the first version to the founder, who approved it for company-wide use, and owned every iteration afterward — including diagnosing why each version eventually broke, and helping every team adopt the next one.

Design Philosophy

Two principles guided every version: start from what actually needs to improve, then design the KPI around it — not the other way around. And a number can never fully capture human effort, feeling, or luck; I tried to build that acknowledgement into the model itself, and say it out loud when presenting it to the team.

Model Evolution

V1 Company-Wide Employee Satisfaction Survey Score, Organisation Health — moved every team beyond raw task counts.
→
V1 — HR HR-Specific Time to Hire, Absenteeism Rate, Attrition Rate — a basic functional layer for HR alone.
→
V2 HR Ratio Model Recruiting & Interview Productivity — pushed quality of hire over volume.
→
V3 Gamified Model One weighted score balancing sourcing effort, shortlisting discipline, and interview outcomes.

Each iteration was a direct response to a real failure mode observed on the ground, not a theoretical redesign.

V2 — What Broke

V2 introduced Recruiting Productivity (RP = candidates passed ÷ shortlisted) and Interview Productivity (IP = interns retained ÷ candidates passed), with goals of RP ≥ 50% and IP ≥ 60%. It backfired: interns became so focused on quality that they started arguing over candidates before even interviewing them, and the model gave no credit for the effort put into sourcing in the first place.

V3 — The Fix

V3 rebuilt scoring as a single weighted formula that rewarded sourcing effort and interview outcomes together, while capping shortlists to keep screening disciplined. Interns no longer had to choose between chasing volume or defending quality — both were already priced into one number.

V3 Scoring Formula

Total Score = 5A + 2B − 6C + 10D − 4E
CodeDefinitionWeight
AJob Ad Links Posted (sourcing)+5 pts each
BApplicants on Those Job Ads+2 pts each
CShortlists Made (min 20, max 60)−6 pts each
DCandidates Passed Interview+10 pts each
ECandidates Not Selected (C − D)−4 pts each

Worked example: a full team of 20 HR interns (5 sub-teams of 4) posting 56 job ads, sourcing 281 applicants, making 60 shortlists, and passing 20 candidates scored 522 — landing in the "Good" band. Score bands: below 330 = Poor, 330–519 = Developing, 520–649 = Good, 650+ = Excellent.

Outcome

The V1 model was adopted company-wide across every department under my oversight. The HR model was iterated three times, each version built in direct response to a real failure mode observed on the ground. The V3 gamified model was easier to understand and adopted enthusiastically — interns reported lower stress and began looking forward to their weekly scores rather than dreading them. Rolled out across 5 teams, it drove a 60% productivity increase, a 40% drop in absenteeism, a 30% cut in time-to-hire, and lifted employee retention from 10% to 40%.

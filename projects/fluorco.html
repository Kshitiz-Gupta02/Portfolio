FluorCo — Diagnosing a Stagnant HF Business

Why a five-decade hydrofluoric acid manufacturer's core business had flatlined despite ~40% company-wide growth — and what a fix could look like.

RoleTeam member, 4-person team
ResultCleared Round 1 of 3
TeamSamaj Sevak, Shiv Nadar University

Context

FluorCo is a ~50-year-old manufacturer of hydrofluoric acid (HF) and other fluorine-based chemicals. Despite a ~40% CAGR across the whole company over the prior three years, its HF business contributed only a third of total sales and had stagnated. The case brief — modelled on a live Bain client engagement — asked for a diagnostic view of the underperformance and a growth strategy, set against global fluorspar supply disruption, fixed industry-wide selling prices, and rising competition from EtchItOn, Glassify, and ElectEtch.

My Role

As part of a 4-person team, I contributed to structuring the MECE issue trees used to diagnose the business, worked through the financial and utilization calculations backing each hypothesis, and helped build out the Round 2 growth-strategy narrative — competitive benchmarking and the final recommendations.

Approach

Round 1 — Diagnostic. Built an issue tree off the objective "how can FluorCo maximize market share over 5 years?" — down the market branch: rising supplier bargaining power from fluorspar disruption, category-mix realignment, and coverage into underrepresented regions. Down the company branch: isolated rising raw-material cost — not fixed costs — as the real margin driver, and traced revenue growth back to customer volume rather than price, since HF pricing has stayed flat industry-wide for a decade. Backed this with a queuing-theory calculation showing what raising utilization from 64% toward a 90% target could do to throughput.

Round 2 — Strategy. Expanded both blueprints into a full growth deck: an executive summary synthesizing four root causes, diagnostics backed by computed exhibits, competitive and regional benchmarking, and five prioritized recommendations — from diversifying raw-material sourcing to raising capacity utilization and customer acquisition.

The Four Root Causes

100%
Import-dependent raw material — global fluorspar disruption strengthened supplier bargaining power, and FluorCo's Vietnam/Canada sourcing carried a cost disadvantage vs. competitors buying from South Africa.
37–64%
Volatile capacity utilization, FY20–FY22 — a single manufacturing plant with swinging utilization keeps unit costs high; fixed costs weren't the driver, underused capacity was.
$0
Pricing flexibility, industry-wide — HF's selling price has stayed constant for a decade with none expected in the next five years, so cost discipline (not pricing power) had to drive margin recovery.
60% / 10%
South India sales concentration vs. new-customer acquisition rate — heavily concentrated distribution with a low new-customer rate versus competitors' 50–80%, capping volume.

Outcome

The diagnosis traced FluorCo's underperformance to four independently verifiable root causes, each backed by a calculation rather than a qualitative claim — cost-per-case, utilization/queuing math, and revenue CAGR. That got the team through Round 1 (MCQ assessment plus issue-tree submission) of Bain Capability Network's three-round national competition. The Round 2 growth-strategy deck was submitted but the team wasn't among those selected to advance to Round 3.

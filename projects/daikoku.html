Daikoku — Trust-First Hiring Marketplace

Co-founding a two-sided marketplace connecting undergraduate students with real project experience to early-stage startups needing fast, verified talent — from idea to incubated prototype in six months.

RoleCo-Founder — Operations, Strategy & Marketing Lead
TimelineJune – December 2022
Team4 co-founders

The Problem

Student side: college curricula stayed largely theoretical, placement cells in Tier-2/3 colleges were slow and unresponsive, and mainstream job portals were flooded with unverified or unpaid internship postings.

Startup side: over 75% of hires happened through personal references because founders didn't trust standard job boards, and early-stage teams had no affordable way to validate ideas or access scaling tools.

What We Built

A student-facing portal with Google/GitHub sign-on, project listings, an embedded workspace, and gamified rewards ("Geek Bucks") tied to verified project completion. A "Swipe Mode" hiring interface letting startup HRs filter and message pre-evaluated candidates directly, backed by SME project ratings and employer verification. Plus a tiered SaaS toolkit for startups — HRM, task tracking, market research, community validation — alongside transactional services like 7-day MVP builds and idea-validation reports.

My Role

As Operations, Strategy & Marketing Lead, I owned scope definition, team coordination, and delivery timelines across the founding team, from initial ideation through to a working prototype and incubation.

I ran the B2B outbound motion — outreach, positioning, and follow-up across 21 target startups. I coordinated cross-functional delivery between web development, XR/Unity, and operations workstreams to keep the prototype on schedule, led the incubation application and onboarding at SNU's Atal Incubation Centre, and defined the go-to-market and roadmap sequencing — student portal launch, B2B outreach, community build, then admin/XR rollout.

Outcome

I stepped away from the team in December 2022 after roughly six months of building — from ideation through incubation to a working prototype and first paid engagement. The rest of the founding team continued for a few more months before the venture wound down. Daikoku remains a formative example of taking a 0-to-1 concept through incubation-stage validation: defining scope, coordinating a small cross-functional team, and running B2B outreach under real deadline pressure.

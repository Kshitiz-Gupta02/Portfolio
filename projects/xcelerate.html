Maruti Suzuki — EV Market Entry Strategy

Designing a market-entry strategy for Maruti Suzuki to launch electric vehicles in India, against Tata Motors' first-mover advantage and patchy charging infrastructure.

Challenge

Design a market entry strategy for Maruti Suzuki to launch electric vehicles in India — accounting for charging infrastructure constraints, supply chain readiness, and the competitive threat of first movers like Tata Motors. From 51,866 individual registrations nationwide (roughly 16,000–25,000 teams across the B-School and E-School tracks), the case advanced through an Online Assessment, an Executive Summary round, and a Detailed Presentation round to reach the Semi-Final — Top 20 overall, 10 per track — short of the Top 5 Grand Finale.

Market Timing & Positioning

We built the case for entry timing on macro signals — rising GDP, the FAME-2 infrastructure push, growing consumer EV demand — weighed against risks like El Niño-driven drought conditions and rising unemployment, with Tata's first-mover advantage flagged as the key pressure to act on. On SWOT, we leaned on Maruti's brand equity, distribution network, and production efficiency as launch strengths, treating its EV-segment and premium-segment gaps as the weaknesses the strategy needed to close.

Consumer Segmentation & Launch Markets

We mapped buyers across two axes — Family / Adventurous / Status-driven vs. Single / Practical / Value-driven — and targeted the mid-range "ideal customer": first-time car buyers seeking feature-rich, comfortable vehicles, priced against rivals like the Tata Nexon EV and MG ZS EV on a price–body type matrix. For launch states, we recommended Delhi and Karnataka, backed by charging-station density data and low EV-to-charging-station ratios signalling headroom for growth, plus tech-savvy, early-adopter demographics.

Go-to-Market & Risk Mitigation

We proposed "Maruti Suzuki eBharat" — positioned around affordability and low running cost, with range-anxiety myth-busting via test drives and a social/UGC campaign (#eBharatRoadTrip). We paired four adoption risks — charging infrastructure, high EV software cost, vehicle depreciation, low tech penetration — with concrete mitigations: leveraging FAME-2, outsourcing software development, retrofitting/trade-in programs, and government–NGO partnerships. We also flagged adjacent revenue streams beyond the vehicle itself: retrofitting old ICE vehicles to EV, home charging installation, and EV-as-a-SaaS plays like fleet management and vehicle-to-grid technology.

Delhi and Karnataka combine high charging-station counts with a low EVs-per-station ratio — more charging headroom relative to existing EVs than Maharashtra, Rajasthan, or UP — which is why we recommended them as launch states, alongside their tech-savvy, early-adopter demographics.

Competitors' Positioning

Our recommendation targeted the mid-range, low-to-mid price SUV/hatchback space (₹9–15L) that Maruti's ideal first-time buyer shops in — a gap between Tata's budget EVs and the premium Hyundai/MG offerings.
